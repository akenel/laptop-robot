What to buy

Part Why Price seen Link
Raspberry Pi Pico 2 H (RP2350, pre-soldered headers) The neck’s small brain. “H” = headers already on, so no soldering. ~CHF 6 (DigiKey CH, ex VAT); EUR 5.95 (Pimoroni) digitec · Pimoroni
MG90S micro servo (metal gear, 180°) Turns the webcam left/right. EUR 5.00, 200+ in stock (Tinytronics) Tinytronics · AZ-Delivery
Micro-USB data cable Laptop to Pico: power + commands. Many cheap cables are charge-only and will not work. a few CHF any shop; you may already own one
Jumper wires, female-to-female (Dupont) Servo plug to the Pico header pins. a few CHF per pack any shop

Total: roughly CHF 15–20.

Wiring (three wires)

Servo wire Pico pin
Brown / black (ground) GND, pin 38
Red (power) VBUS (5 V from USB), pin 40
Orange / yellow (signal) GP15, pin 20

Software (two halves)

  1. On the Pico, once: flash MicroPython (drag the .uf2 file onto the Pico), then save a short main.py that reads an angle over USB serial and moves the servo. It runs by itself on every power-up. Memory is no issue: 4 MB flash, 520 KB RAM on the Pico 2.
  2. On the laptop: a driver in drivers/ that sends “turn to 90” over USB serial, plus devices/neck.yaml. The MCP server picks it up from the registry.

What I could not verify (honest gaps)

  • Prices on digitec.ch: the pages timed out when fetched. The digitec links come from a search and exist, but price and stock were not read. Check before ordering.
  • Digitec MG90S / jumper wires: not confirmed that they stock them. Tinytronics (NL) and AZ-Delivery (DE) do; postage to Switzerland is not checked.
  • Power: an MG90S can draw several hundred mA when it stalls; a USB port gives ~500 mA. For a light webcam and slow moves this should be fine. If the Pico resets when the servo moves, add a separate 5 V supply (shared ground). Measure, don’t assume.
  • Correction: during the chat Grace said “the original Pico has 2 MB / 264 KB”. True for the Pico 1; the recommended Pico 2 has 4 MB / 520 KB.
